# Figure wishlist: psychology-how-minds-work, lessons 1 to 10

Figures the story lessons would use if the course had them. Each screen teaches without its figure.

## correlation-and-cause

- Step `a-number-for-the-pattern`. A scatter plot generated for a chosen correlation. The learner drags a slider from r = −1 to r = +1 and watches the same 30 dots tighten into a line, spread into a cloud, and flip from rising to falling. Caption: "Drag r from −1 to +1. The sign sets the direction of the slope and the size sets how tightly the dots follow a line."
- Step `how-much-they-share`. Two overlapping bars for the variation in each measure, with the overlap sized by r². The learner changes r and watches the overlap grow. Caption: "The overlap is the share of variation the two measures have in common. At r = 0.4 it is 16%."

## experiments-and-assignment

- Step `what-people-expect`. A trial flow diagram: volunteers, then a random draw, then a drug group and a placebo group, then the measured improvement in each. The learner switches the placebo group off and sees which part of the improvement can no longer be separated. Caption: "With a placebo group the drug's own effect is the gap between the two bars. Without one, expectation and time are mixed in."

## effect-size-and-replication

- Step `how-many-people`. A curve of participants needed per group against d, from 0.2 to 1.0, with a marker the learner moves along it. Caption: "The people needed per group fall as 16 ÷ d². Halving d needs four times as many."

## signal-detection

- Step `four-outcomes`. A two-by-two table of hits, misses, false alarms and correct rejections, filled in from a guard's twenty nights. The learner changes the counts and watches the two rates update. Caption: "Rows show whether a thief came and columns whether the guard shouted. Each rate divides by its own row."

## attention-and-multitasking

- Step `the-gorilla`. A short clip, or a still with a gorilla outline, from a pass-counting film. The learner counts passes on one run and watches freely on another. Caption: "Count the passes made by the white team. The gorilla crosses the middle of the screen."

## perception-as-inference

- Step `one-sense-wobbles`. A dot plot of twelve repeated readings of one cue, piled up around the true height. The learner drags an SD slider from 1 mm to 6 mm and watches the dots spread out. Caption: "Each dot is one look at the block. A small SD packs the dots tightly and a large SD scatters them."

## working-memory

- Step `holding-a-list`. A short presentation of list A and list B, with a four-slot tray. The learner steps through the digits and watches slots fill and overflow for list A, while list B occupies one slot as a familiar run. Caption: "List A fills every slot after four digits. List B is one familiar run and takes a single slot."

## forgetting-and-spacing

- Step `testing-beats-rereading`. Two recall curves for the rereading group and the recall-practice group, from five minutes to one week, crossing over at some point in between. The learner moves a marker along the time axis and reads off both scores. Caption: "Rereading leads after five minutes. After a week the group that practised recall is ahead."

## conditioning

- Step `the-dog-and-the-bell`. A strip of cartoon frames across days: bell then food on days one and two, then the bell alone on day three with drooling shown. The learner scrubs through the days. Caption: "On day three the bell comes alone and the dog still drools."

## base-rates

- Step `the-tempting-answer`. Two bars side by side for the number of farmers and the number of librarians in a town, with a marker for how many of each fit the description of Steve. The learner changes the proportion who fit and reads off the chance that Steve is a librarian. Caption: "Many more farmers than librarians means a shy farmer is easier to find than a shy librarian, even when the description fits librarians better."

## anchors-and-intuition

- Step `first-number`. The wheel of fortune from Tversky and Kahneman, with a spinner that stops at 10 or 65 and a box showing the median guess for each group. The learner picks the stop and sees the typical guess move. Caption: "Pick where the wheel stops. The question about Africa has nothing to do with the wheel, yet the typical guess follows it."

## social-influence

- Step `matching-lines`. An Asch card: one standard line, three comparison lines, and a row of confederates giving the same wrong answer before the volunteer's turn. The learner switches the confederates on and off. Caption: "With no one else answering, the match is easy. With a unanimous wrong majority, about 37 critical answers in every 100 go along."
