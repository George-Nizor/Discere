# Figures wished for: Maths Foundations lessons 1 to 3

Every screen below teaches without the figure. It uses `number_machine` or `equation_balance` where one fits, and the
entries here are what would help more. Nothing in the app was changed for them.

What the two existing figures cannot do is act. `number_machine` runs one way, from the input to the output, and
`equation_balance` lets the learner try values of x but cannot apply a move to both pans. Most of the wishes below
come from that.

## Lesson 1: what-a-letter-stands-for

### the-game-of-the-hidden-number
- **Shows:** a machine whose input is a "?" box and whose output is given, with a button that runs it backwards one
  box at a time, so "add 8, got 14" turns into "take away 8, get 6".
- **Learner changes:** the round (add, subtract, double, halve and a number) and the step button.
- **Caption:** "Press the button to undo the round one step at a time, starting from the answer."

### a-name-for-the-hidden-number
- **Shows:** one sentence in three forms, side by side: "my number + 5 = 12", then "□ + 5 = 12", then "x + 5 = 12",
  with the hidden number highlighted and carried across each form.
- **Learner changes:** the letter (x, n, m or a), and the sentence rewrites itself.
- **Caption:** "The same sentence three ways. Pick a letter and watch the gap take its name."

### one-letter-one-number (worked example)
- **Shows:** x + x + 4 with both x's lit, a slider for x, and a line below that fills in each x and then the sum.
- **Learner changes:** the value of x.
- **Caption:** "Both x's change together. Move the slider and the sum follows."

### three-lots-of-x
- **Shows:** a bar model. Three equal bars each labelled x, stacked end to end into one long bar labelled 3x, with the
  total printed under it.
- **Learner changes:** the length of x with a slider.
- **Caption:** "3x is three bars of length x laid end to end."

### which-step-goes-first
- **Shows:** two machines for 3x − 4 side by side. The left multiplies first and then subtracts. The right subtracts
  first and then multiplies. Their outputs are printed and the left one is marked as the convention.
- **Learner changes:** the value of x.
- **Caption:** "The same two steps in two orders give different answers. The rule picks the left machine."

## Lesson 2: keeping-the-balance

### what-keeps-the-scales-level
- **Shows:** a pair of scales with a tied bag and loose marbles in the left pan and a heap in the right pan. The beam
  tips or levels as marbles move.
- **Learner changes:** lifts marbles out of either pan, one at a time or in groups, and watches the beam.
- **Caption:** "Take marbles out of one pan only and the scales tip. Take the same number out of both and they stay level."

### an-equation-is-a-balance, two-moves-on-the-scales and solve-by-balancing
- **Shows:** `equation_balance` with the equation written above the pans and a move panel: an operation (add, subtract,
  multiply, divide) and a number. Choosing a move applies it to both pans, redraws the pans and adds a line to a
  running list of equations. Bags are drawn as bags and split into equal groups when the learner divides.
- **Learner changes:** the move. A one-sided option is available so the learner can see the beam tip.
- **Caption:** "Choose a move and it is done to both sides. The list underneath is your working."

### when-the-numbers-go-below-zero
- **Shows:** a number line from −12 to 4 with a marker at −10 that hops up 4 to −6 when the learner presses
  "add 4", and a thermometer drawn beside it.
- **Learner changes:** the amount added and the starting point.
- **Caption:** "Adding 4 to −10 moves up the line to −6."

## Lesson 3: undoing-in-the-right-order

### running-a-calculation-backwards
- **Shows:** the existing machine with a forwards and backwards switch. In backwards mode the output is the input of
  the run and each box shows its opposite (× 3 becomes ÷ 3). A second, greyed run shows the wrong order ending at 39.
- **Learner changes:** the direction, and whether to show the wrong-order run.
- **Caption:** "Run the machine backwards, last box first. The grey run shows what happens in the wrong order."

### brackets-make-a-package
- **Shows:** 3(x + 2) drawn as a parcel. A closed box labelled "x + 2" sits inside a wrapper labelled "× 3". Pressing
  "undo" opens the wrapper first and then the box.
- **Learner changes:** the number of layers (one, two or three) and the undo button.
- **Caption:** "Open the outside first. The last job is the outer wrapper."

### answers-need-not-be-whole
- **Shows:** a number line from 0 to 5 with whole numbers marked, a zoomed strip between 2 and 3, and the solution
  2.8 and its fraction 14/5 shown as one point.
- **Learner changes:** the zoom, and a switch between fraction and decimal.
- **Caption:** "The solution sits between 2 and 3. Zoom in and it lands on 2.8, which is the fraction 14/5."

## Lesson 4: a-point-is-a-pair

`coordinate_plane` shows points and a line, and nothing moves. The wishes below are about walking and counting.

### directions-from-the-camp
- **Shows:** the grid with the origin marked as the camp. Two buttons, "across" and "up", walk a marker one square at a
  time, counting aloud on the grid, and end on the labelled spot.
- **Learner changes:** the two numbers of the pair, and which part of the walk is playing.
- **Caption:** "Press across, then up. The marker stops at the spot the pair names."

### which-number-comes-first
- **Shows:** two pairs side by side with the numbers swapped, such as (4, 1) and (1, 4), each with its own walk drawn as
  an L-shaped path from the origin, in two colours.
- **Learner changes:** the two numbers, and a swap button.
- **Caption:** "The same two numbers in the other order end at a different spot."

### past-the-camp
- **Shows:** the same walk with a sign switch on each number. Flipping a sign turns that part of the walk round and
  moves the marker into another corner of the grid.
- **Learner changes:** the sign of each number.
- **Caption:** "A minus sign sends that part of the walk the other way."

## Lesson 5: how-steep-is-it

### steepness-as-a-number
- **Shows:** the two hill paths drawn as sloping lines over the ground, each with its climb and its ground length
  labelled and the quotient printed beside it (2 and 0.5).
- **Learner changes:** the climb and the ground length of each path.
- **Caption:** "Share the climb over the ground. The bigger the share, the steeper the path."

### rise-and-run and subtracting-in-the-same-order
- **Shows:** `coordinate_plane` with a staircase drawn between two points: a horizontal step labelled with the run and a
  vertical step labelled with the rise, and the gradient printed as rise ÷ run.
- **Learner changes:** drags either point, or switches the order of the two points to see the signs of rise and run both
  flip.
- **Caption:** "Drag a point. The run goes across and the rise goes up, and their quotient is the gradient."

## Lesson 6: from-equation-to-line

### the-candle-on-a-graph
- **Shows:** a graph whose vertical scale reaches 14, so the candle's starting height of 12 appears as a point at
  (0, 12). `coordinate_plane` stops at 10 and clips the line there.
- **Learner changes:** the hour with a slider, and the candle drawn beside the graph shortens as the point moves.
- **Caption:** "Each hour the candle is 2 centimetres shorter, and the point steps down the line."

### writing-the-line-as-an-equation
- **Shows:** `coordinate_plane` with the equation y = mx + c written above it and two sliders. The line redraws as the
  sliders move, and the point (0, c) is marked.
- **Learner changes:** m and c.
- **Caption:** "Move m and the line tilts. Move c and it slides up or down the y-axis."

### drawing-a-line-from-its-equation
- **Shows:** a small table of x and y for the equation, filled in one row at a time, with each row plotted as it is
  completed and the line drawn once two rows exist.
- **Learner changes:** the x values to try.
- **Caption:** "Pick an x, work out y, and plot the pair. Two points fix the line."
