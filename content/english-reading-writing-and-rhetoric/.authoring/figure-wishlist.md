# Figures wished for: English lessons 1 to 5

Every screen below teaches without the figure. Each uses `language_explorer` (clauses, voice, compress, toulmin or
passage) where one fits, and the entries here are what would help more. Nothing in the app was changed for them.

The explorer shows a reading that is already worked out. What it cannot do is let the learner change the text and watch
the reading change, and most of the wishes below come from that.

## Lesson 1: clause-anatomy

### word-that-moves
- **Shows:** one sentence, "She opens the window", with a switch between present and past that rewrites the sentence and
  highlights the one word that changes.
- **Learner changes:** the time (present or past) and the sentence, from a short list.
- **Caption:** "Switch the time and watch which word has to change."

## Lesson 3: actors-and-actions

### one-sentence-three-edits
- **Shows:** "The delivery of the parcels was completed by the courier on Monday" as a single line that rewrites itself in
  three stages (actor first, verb unburied, padding cut), with a running word count beside it.
- **Learner changes:** a button that applies the next edit.
- **Caption:** "Press Next edit to apply each repair and read the word count."

The compress model can show one pass of cuts and replacements on one fixed sentence. It cannot reorder a passive into an
active, so the first edit is only described in the prose.

## Lesson 4: claim-grounds-warrant

### the-unstated-rule
- **Shows:** the Toulmin diagram with arrows from the grounds to the claim and an empty slot between them, which fills
  with the warrant when the learner presses a button.
- **Learner changes:** whether the warrant is shown, so the gap is visible before it is filled.
- **Caption:** "The arrow only works if something carries you across. Press Show the rule to see what."

## Lesson 5: appeals-and-occasion

### date-the-opening
- **Shows:** a number line from 1776 to 1863 with a bracket that the learner can stretch, labelled in score-and-years
  ("4 score and 7 years").
- **Learner changes:** the length of the bracket in years.
- **Caption:** "Stretch the bracket back from 1863 until it spans four score and seven years."

### three-ways-to-persuade
- **Shows:** a passage in which the learner picks one appeal at a time (ethos, pathos or logos) and only the spans of that
  appeal stay highlighted.
- **Learner changes:** which appeal is highlighted.
- **Caption:** "Choose an appeal to see which parts of the speech work that way."

# Figures wished for: English lessons 6 to 10

Every screen below teaches without the figure. The sonnet model needs exactly fourteen lines, the scansion model plays a
fixed pulse and the passage model shows a reading that is already worked out, so what is missing is mostly a figure that
the learner can change.

## Lesson 6: figures-of-speech

### turning-the-words-round
- **Shows:** a sentence in two halves with its key words as movable chips, and lines between matching words that redraw
  as the chips move.
- **Learner changes:** the order of the key words in the second half.
- **Caption:** "Drag the words of the second half into reverse order and watch the lines cross."

## Lesson 7: metre-and-scansion

### syllables-and-beats
- **Shows:** a nursery-rhyme line with a pad the learner taps, and the stressed syllables lighting up beside each tap.
- **Learner changes:** the taps, at their own pace.
- **Caption:** "Tap along to the line. The marked syllables are the ones your taps should land on."

## Lesson 8: sound-and-image

### consonants-that-repeat
- **Shows:** a line typed or picked by the learner, with every word that opens with the same sound joined by an arc.
- **Learner changes:** the line, from a short list, or a sound to follow.
- **Caption:** "Pick a sound and see which words in the line open with it."

## Lesson 9: sonnet-and-volta

### letters-for-rhymes
- **Shows:** a short verse of four to six lines, each end-word given a letter as the learner taps a rhyming pair.
- **Learner changes:** which end-words are paired.
- **Caption:** "Tap two end-words that rhyme and they take the same letter."

The sonnet model cannot show fewer than fourteen lines, so this screen has no figure.

### the-italian-form
- **Shows:** one fourteen-line sonnet with a switch that brackets the lines as four, four, four and two, or as eight and six.
- **Learner changes:** the grouping, and the line where the turn sits.
- **Caption:** "Switch between the two ways of dividing the lines and see where the turn falls in each."

## Lesson 10: point-of-view

### into-a-character-s-head
- **Shows:** one thought of a character with a slider that moves it from direct speech, through reported thought, to free
  indirect discourse, highlighting the words of person and tense that change at each stop.
- **Learner changes:** the slider.
- **Caption:** "Move the slider and watch the pronoun and the tense change while the exclamation stays."

# Figures wished for: English lessons 11 and 12

Every screen below teaches without the figure. The irony model shows one line with what it says and what is known, and the
arc model shows scenes that are already drawn. Neither lets the learner move anything.

## Lesson 11: irony-and-reliability

### a-remark-that-means-the-opposite
- **Shows:** the spilt-soup remark with a slider that moves the scene from "soup in his lap" to "nothing has happened", and
  a marker that shows the remark reading as praise or as irony.
- **Learner changes:** the slider, which removes or restores the facts that let the listener hear the gap.
- **Caption:** "Remove the stain and the remark turns into plain thanks. Put it back and the gap returns."

### praise-that-turns
- **Shows:** Antony's speech as a column of lines, with a meter beside the phrase "an honourable man" that moves from
  respect to accusation as each piece of evidence is added.
- **Learner changes:** how many facts about Caesar the crowd has heard, from none to all.
- **Caption:** "Add the facts one at a time and watch the same words change their meaning."

## Lesson 12: structure-and-scenes

### a-story-as-a-hill
- **Shows:** a row of scene bars the learner can drag up and down, with the line through them and the highest bar marked
  as the climax.
- **Learner changes:** the height of each scene.
- **Caption:** "Drag the bars and see the climax move to whichever scene is highest."

### scenes-that-turn
- **Shows:** a row of scenes, each with an opening and a closing sign that the learner can flip, and a running count of the
  scenes that turn.
- **Learner changes:** the opening and closing sign of each scene.
- **Caption:** "Flip a sign and watch the count of turning scenes change."
