# Figure wishlist: biology-cells-to-ecosystems

Figures the story lessons would use if the course had them. Each screen listed here teaches without its figure, using the existing `biology_explorer` diagram (cell and membrane models) instead. Lessons covered so far: `inside-a-cell`, `diffusion-and-membranes`, `water-across-a-membrane`, and every later lesson up to `ecological-evidence`.

The existing cell figure lists every part in its Inspect menu from the first screen, including parts a lesson has not named yet. Wherever a screen below asks for labels that appear as they are introduced, that is the reason.

## inside-a-cell

### the-edge-of-the-cell

- Shows: a cell as a bag with its plasma membrane, and particles of food, oxygen and waste around it. Some particles cross, others bounce off.
- Learner changes: which substance to send at the cell, and which way it is travelling (in or out).
- Caption: "Send different substances at the cell. The plasma membrane lets some through and turns others away."

### where-the-instructions-are-kept

- Shows: the same strand of DNA drawn in two cells. In the eukaryotic cell it sits inside a nucleus with its own membrane, and in the bacterium it lies loose in the nucleoid. Part names appear only after the lesson has introduced them.
- Learner changes: which cell is shown, and which part is highlighted.
- Caption: "The same DNA kept in two ways: behind a membrane of its own in a nucleus, or loose in a nucleoid."

### what-a-leaf-cell-adds

- Shows: a leaf cell with a light switch. With the light on, the chloroplasts catch light energy. With it off, they stop, and the mitochondria carry on working on sugar.
- Learner changes: the light switch, and a toggle between a leaf cell and a root cell (the root cell has no chloroplasts).
- Caption: "Switch the light off. The chloroplasts stop catching light, and the mitochondria carry on."
- Note: use it on the teaching screen only. The next screen asks what keeps a leaf cell going after dark.

### a-wall-outside

- Shows: a cell swelling inside a stiff wall, like a balloon in a box, with the plasma membrane inside the wall and water and dissolved substances passing through the wall's gaps.
- Learner changes: how much water has entered.
- Caption: "As the cell fills, it presses on the wall, and the wall presses back."

## diffusion-and-membranes

### two-rooms-and-an-open-door

- Shows: two equal rooms joined by a doorway, with dots wandering at random. Counters show the people in each room, the people who crossed each way in the last minute, and the net movement.
- Learner changes: the starting numbers in each room, the share of each room that crosses per minute, and a Step button for one minute at a time (or Play).
- Caption: "Step through the minutes. Count the people crossing each way and watch the net movement shrink as the rooms even out."

### why-nothing-seems-to-change

- Shows: the two-compartment membrane figure at equilibrium, with one particle marked and two running counters for crossings left to right and right to left.
- Learner changes: which particle is marked, and the starting amounts, so a balanced case and an unbalanced case can be compared.
- Caption: "At equilibrium the two counters rise at the same rate, so the amount on each side stays put."

### what-the-membrane-lets-through

- Shows: a membrane with two substances in the compartments, water and a dye. The existing figure can pass a solute, water or neither, but cannot show two substances at once.
- Learner changes: which of the two substances the membrane is permeable to.
- Caption: "Choose what the membrane lets through. A substance moves only if the membrane is permeable to it."

## water-across-a-membrane

### how-strong-a-solution-is

- Shows: a cup or jug with a litre scale and spoonfuls of syrup. A readout gives the amount of syrup, the volume, and amount ÷ volume.
- Learner changes: spoonfuls of syrup added, and litres of water added.
- Caption: "Add syrup or water. The concentration is the number of spoonfuls divided by the number of litres."

### following-the-numbers

- Shows: the existing water membrane figure, with each side's volume and concentration printed beside its solute amount, and with unequal starting volumes allowed. The current figure shows solute amounts only and always starts with equal volumes.
- Learner changes: the solute on each side, the starting volumes, and the slider for water crossing.
- Caption: "Move the slider. The solute amounts stay fixed, while each side's volume and concentration change."

### pressure-from-the-cell-wall

- Shows: a plant cell in a bowl, with a pressure gauge on the cell wall. In plain water the cell fills until the wall pushes back. In salty water the cell loses water and shrinks away from the wall. A leaf made of such cells is shown crisp or limp.
- Learner changes: how salty the bowl is.
- Caption: "In plain water the cell fills until the wall pushes back. In salty water it loses water and pulls away from the wall."

## enzymes-and-evidence

### the-dent

- Shows: an enzyme as a folded shape with a dent (the active site) and a substrate shaped to fit it. The product leaves and the next substrate moves in. A second substrate with a different shape does not fit.
- Learner changes: which substrate is offered, and a temperature slider that bends the enzyme's shape until the substrate no longer fits.
- Caption: "Offer different substrates. Only the one that matches the active site binds, and heat that bends the enzyme stops the fit."
- Note: the screen `why-shape-matters` teaches this in words only, using no figure.

### the-push-a-reaction-needs

- Shows: a hill and a ball (activation energy), drawn twice: one hill high without an enzyme, one low with an enzyme, with a count of molecules that clear it each minute.
- Learner changes: whether the enzyme is present.
- Caption: "Switch the enzyme on. The hill gets lower, so more molecules get over it each minute."
- Note: the screen currently uses the two-beaker product graph instead.

## light-to-sugar

### building-sugar

- Shows: a leaf cell with carbon dioxide and water entering, light shining on it, and glucose and oxygen leaving, with the atoms colour-coded so the carbon can be followed from CO2 into glucose.
- Learner changes: the number of glucose molecules built (1 to 5), which scales the molecule counts.
- Caption: "Follow the carbon atoms from carbon dioxide into the glucose."
- Note: the existing energy figure shows only per-glucose totals for one to three glucose.

## fuel-to-cell-work

### where-the-energy-goes

- Shows: a bar for the energy released from one glucose, split into the part captured in ATP and the part given off as heat, with a mitochondrion as the place where it happens.
- Learner changes: nothing, or a toggle between a candle (all heat and light) and a cell (small steps, part captured).
- Caption: "A candle gives the energy out at once. A cell releases it in steps and captures part of it in ATP."

## copying-dna

### unzipping-and-rebuilding

- Shows: a DNA molecule that unzips down its length, each old strand gaining new bases one at a time until there are two molecules, each with one old and one new strand (old strands in one colour, new in another).
- Learner changes: a Step button that advances the copying one base at a time.
- Caption: "Step through the copying. Each old strand picks up new bases by the pairing rule, so every molecule ends up half old and half new."
- Note: the existing DNA figure shows one strand and its partner, and the division figure shows counts, not the unzipping.

## mitosis-and-growth

### sharing-out-the-copies

- Shows: a cell with its chromosomes as joined pairs of sister chromatids lining up in the middle, being pulled apart to opposite ends, and the cell pinching into two daughter cells.
- Learner changes: a slider through the stages of one division.
- Caption: "Move through the division. Each chromosome's two sister chromatids are pulled to opposite ends, so each daughter cell gets a full set."
- Note: the existing division figure shows only the start and end states with counts.

## meiosis-and-gametes

### copy-once-divide-twice

- Shows: a cell with three homologous pairs, drawn as chromosomes that can be told apart by colour (one of each pair from the mother, one from the father). Pairs line up in the middle, the two chromosomes of each pair are pulled to opposite ends, then the sister chromatids are pulled apart in the second division.
- Learner changes: a slider through the stages of meiosis I and II.
- Caption: "Move through the two divisions. In the first, the chromosomes of each pair go to different cells. In the second, the sister chromatids separate."
- Note: the existing division figure shows only the four states with counts, and its chromosomes cannot be told apart, so it cannot show which chromosome of a pair goes where.

### why-no-two-gametes-are-alike

- Shows: three pairs of coloured chromosomes and the gametes they can make. Each pair can send either chromosome to a gamete, and the figure lists the 8 combinations. A second view shows one pair swapping a segment (crossing over).
- Learner changes: a button that draws a random gamete, and a toggle for crossing over.
- Caption: "Draw gamete after gamete. Each pair sends one chromosome at random, so the combinations differ, and crossing over mixes the chromosomes themselves."
- Note: the screen teaches in words and numbers only.

## predicting-inheritance

### a-chance-for-each-child

- Shows: the Aa × Aa square with a row of seeds that grow into purple or white plants one at a time, and a running count of white seeds against the 25% expected.
- Learner changes: how many seeds are drawn (4, 40, 400), and a Draw button.
- Caption: "Draw seeds from the cross. With a few seeds the white share jumps about, and with many it settles near 25%."
- Note: the existing cross figure shows the square and its expected shares only.

## mutation-and-variation

### a-change-in-the-bases

- Shows: a short strand copied base by base, with a slip that swaps one base (substitution), adds one (insertion) or misses one out (deletion), and the length of the copy next to the original.
- Learner changes: which kind of slip happens, and where.
- Caption: "Choose a slip. A substitution keeps the length, while an insertion or a deletion changes it."
- Note: the existing DNA figure shows one strand and its partner. It cannot show a copy that differs from the original.

## natural-selection

### what-the-birds-did

- Shows: a meadow of blue and gold beetles. Birds take beetles at a rate set for each colour, and a bar for each colour shows its share of the meadow before and after, then in the next generation.
- Learner changes: the share of each colour the birds eat, and the colour of the grass.
- Caption: "Change what the birds eat. The colour they find easier to see loses share, and the young inherit the new shares."
- Note: the existing population figure shows only a before and an after share.

## reading-food-webs

### a-change-moves-along-the-arrows

- Shows: the pond web with one organism that can be removed or increased, and an arrow on each link that turns up or down to show which way the change pushes its neighbours.
- Learner changes: which organism is changed, and whether it rises or falls.
- Caption: "Change one organism. Its food tends to rise when it falls, and its eaters tend to fall, but the diagram shows only the direction."
- Note: the existing food web figure draws the links only.

## energy-between-levels

### where-the-energy-goes

- Shows: one meal drawn as a bar, split into the part never eaten, the part not digested, the part used for living and given off as heat, and the part built into new body. A second bar shows the new body as the share the next animal can eat.
- Learner changes: the transfer efficiency, so the new-body share grows or shrinks.
- Caption: "Change the efficiency. Only the part built into new body is left for the next animal to eat."
- Note: the screen teaches in words only. The existing pyramid figure appears on the next screens.

## population-limits

### doubling-every-day

- Shows: the 20 square metre pond from above, with the lily patch drawn at its area for the chosen day, next to a count of the area and the amount added that day.
- Learner changes: the day, with a toggle between doubling every day and a limited patch that levels off.
- Caption: "Move through the days. The patch adds more each day, until the pond runs out of room."
- Note: the existing graph figure plots the area against the day, but cannot show the pond itself.

### births-and-deaths

- Shows: a population of 50 dots with the births added and the deaths removed one at a time, and a running net change and size.
- Learner changes: the number of births and the number of deaths.
- Caption: "Set the births and the deaths. The net change is births minus deaths, and the size moves by that amount."

## ecological-evidence

### a-control-and-a-fair-draw

- Shows: a row of twelve pots on a windowsill, some nearer the window, shared between a fertiliser group and a control group. One view picks the pots by hand and one picks at random, with the average light of each group beside it.
- Learner changes: a button that deals the pots again at random, and a toggle for choosing by hand.
- Caption: "Deal the pots again. Chosen by hand, one group gets more light. Dealt by chance, the groups come out alike."

### many-pots

- Shows: each pot's growth as a dot on a line, with the mean marked, for two sets of three pots that share the same mean and differ in spread.
- Learner changes: the growth of each pot, by dragging its dot.
- Caption: "Drag the dots. The mean can stay put while the spread changes."
