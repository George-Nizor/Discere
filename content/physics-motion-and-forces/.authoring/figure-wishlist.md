# Figure wishlist: Physics: Motion and Forces

Figures the first five lessons would use if the course had the diagram types. Each screen teaches without them.

## measuring-motion, step moving-the-origin

Shows: a track with two tapes drawn one above the other, the second tape's zero shifted along the floor. A walker moves from a start mark to a finish mark and both tapes read the two positions.

The learner can change: where the second tape's zero sits, using a slider.

Caption: "Slide the second tape's zero. Every reading changes by the same amount, and the displacement stays the same."

## reading-motion-graphs, step time-along-position-up

Shows: a table of times and positions beside a position–time graph that fills in one dot per row as the learner steps through the table.

The learner can change: which row is plotted next, and the walker's pace from a short list.

Caption: "Each row of the table becomes one dot. Equal steps in position give dots in a straight line."

## predicting-a-motion, step distance-when-velocity-changes

Shows: a velocity–time graph with the area under the line shaded, next to the same area redrawn as a rectangle at the average velocity.

The learner can change: the starting velocity and the acceleration, using sliders.

Caption: "The sloping line and the level line enclose the same area, so both cover the same distance."

## falling-and-rising, step how-far-it-falls

Shows: a strobe picture of a dropped stone, one image per second beside a vertical ruler, with the gap between neighbouring images marked.

The learner can change: the drop height and the number of flashes shown.

Caption: "The gaps between the images grow each second. Measured from the start, the distances are 5 m, 20 m, 45 m and 80 m."

## balanced-forces, step a-tug-of-war

Shows: a rope with a flag at its midpoint and one arrow on each side, each arrow as long as its team's pull, and a third arrow below showing what is left over.

The learner can change: each team's pull, using two sliders.

Caption: "Slide either pull. The short arrow underneath is what the two leave over, and the flag moves towards the longer pull."

## pairs-of-forces, step pairs-on-different-objects

Shows: a book on a table, the Earth beneath, and four arrows: the weight and the support force on the book, the book's push on the table and the book's pull on the Earth. Picking one arrow highlights its partner.

The learner can change: which arrow is picked.

Caption: "Pick an arrow. Its partner is highlighted on the other object. The weight and the support force both sit on the book, so neither is the other's partner."

## friction-and-motion, step the-limit

Shows: a graph with the push along the bottom and the friction up the side. Friction follows a rising diagonal while the box is still, peaks at the static limit, then drops to a flat line at the sliding value once the box moves.

The learner can change: the push, using a slider, and the two coefficients of friction.

Caption: "Slide the push up. Friction rises with it until the limit, then drops to a smaller steady value as the box starts to slide."

## work-by-a-force, step force-times-distance

Shows: a graph of force against distance for a steady force, with the rectangle under the line shaded and its area labelled in joules.

The learner can change: the force and the distance, using two sliders.

Caption: "The shaded rectangle is the work. Make the force or the distance larger and the area grows in step."

## energy-of-motion, step the-formula

Shows: two bikes side by side with brake marks on the road, one at speed v and one at twice v, and a bar for each showing its kinetic energy.

The learner can change: the speed of each bike, using sliders.

Caption: "Double the speed and the bar grows to four times the height, so the marks on the road are four times as long."

## lifting-and-falling-energy, step energy-stored-in-height

Shows: a box beside a height scale with a bar for its potential energy that grows as the box is raised and shrinks as it is lowered, and a second bar for the zero of height.

The learner can change: the height of the box and where the scale starts from.

Caption: "Raise the box and the bar grows. Move the zero of the scale and the heights change, but a lift of the same size gives the same change in the bar."

## keeping-energy-account, step the-whole-account

Shows: a stacked bar chart of kinetic, potential and thermal energy at several points down a ramp, with the total drawn as a line that stays level.

The learner can change: the height of the ramp and how rough the surface is.

Caption: "Make the ramp rougher and thermal energy takes a bigger share of the bar. The total height of the stack never changes."

## power-and-efficiency, step the-useful-share

Shows: an energy flow from left to right. One wide band for the input energy splits into a band for the useful output and a thinner band for the wasted energy, each labelled in joules.

The learner can change: the input energy and the useful share, using two sliders.

Caption: "Slide the useful share. The band that reaches the water gets wider or thinner, and the two bands always add up to the input."

## momentum-and-impulse, step a-longer-stop

Shows: a force–time graph of a catch with the area under the curve shaded. A short, tall curve is drawn over a long, low one, and both shaded areas are labelled with the same impulse.

The learner can change: the stopping time, using a slider.

Caption: "Stretch the stopping time. The curve gets lower and wider and the shaded area, the impulse, stays the same."

## carts-that-stick, step sharing-the-total

Shows: two carts on a track before and after the crash, each with an arrow whose length is its momentum, and a bar beside each picture showing the total momentum and the total kinetic energy.

The learner can change: the mass and starting velocity of each cart, using sliders.

Caption: "Change either cart. The momentum bars are equal before and after the crash, and the kinetic energy bar is shorter afterwards."
