# New subjects, prioritised

A proposal of 14 November 2026 for completing the library's breadth, for a learner who wants to be
well rounded across the main fields of knowledge. Nothing here is started; George chooses what goes
ahead and in what order. Once a course is written it leaves this list and enters
`docs/content-ledger.json`. Sources and their licences are in `docs/authoring/open-sources.md`.

## How the gaps were found

The 67 courses were set against two maps:

- **The broad fields of knowledge** a general education covers: matter and energy, the earth, life,
  the human body and mind, society and how it is governed, money and work, the arts, religion and
  belief, history, languages, and the tools of thought (mathematics, logic, philosophy).
- **Atlas Open Academy's catalogue** (315 courses in 39 subjects, read on 14 November). Atlas's own
  lesson text has no reuse licence, so it is used only as a map and for the sources it cites.

The library is strong on computing, software, data and AI (about half the courses), broad on
mathematics, science and engineering, and thin or empty on society, money, law, the arts, religion,
modern history and languages. Atlas has courses in all of those.

Each candidate below was weighed on four things: how large a gap it fills for a well-rounded adult,
how much it carries over to other fields and to ordinary life, whether good open sources exist,
and how well Discere's question types suit it (marked on the computer: choices, numbers, typed
answers, order, sort, quoted sources side by side, code).

## Later, or as modules of the above

- **The era courses after the Ancient World:** the medieval world, the early modern world
  (1450 to 1750), the twentieth century. With course 1 they complete a run through world history.
- **Philosophy of Science:** how science explains, confirms and goes wrong. Rebus Introduction to
  Philosophy (CC BY).
- **Music History and Listening:** from chant to the present, and other traditions. Understanding
  Music (BY-SA). Pairs with Music Theory.
- **Proof and Discrete Mathematics:** how mathematicians argue. Sundstrom, Mathematical Reasoning
  (NC-SA). Extends Logic.
- **Public Health and Epidemiology:** partly covered by Statistical and Causal Inference.
- **Classical Mythology,** and **Greek and Latin Roots of English** (a quick vocabulary win).
- **Genetics and Evolution, Neuroscience, Organic Chemistry:** deeper science courses after the
  current introductions.
- **Film, History of Architecture, Marketing and Management, Criminology:** useful, lower priority.

## What each needs from the app

- Courses 1 to 11 and 13 to 15 fit the existing question types. History, religion, literature,
  law and politics use quoted extracts side by side; geography and history want maps (Discere has
  no map component yet, so maps would be drawn figures); art history needs images, which the
  existing Wikimedia retriever handles with credit and licence.
- A language course is a bigger build. It needs listening (audio of real speakers, or the
  browser's speech, whose quality varies by language), a sentence-building question (the order
  question with words as items), and typed answers that accept accents and word order variants.
  It is a sequence of courses, not one.

## Decisions for George

- **Which of the first eight, and in what order.** The order above is a recommendation.
- **Which country** for Law and Personal Finance. The open sources are mostly American; tax,
  pensions and much of the law differ by country, and the courses should teach the rules George
  lives under.
- **Which language,** if any, and whether listening matters enough to build audio first.

## Cost

A new course takes a source pack, a plan, three lesson writers and an integration step
(`docs/authoring/README.md`). By estimate (usage was not measured per course), each of the last
two took one to two per cent of the weekly usage limit, so the first eight would take roughly ten
to fifteen per cent.
