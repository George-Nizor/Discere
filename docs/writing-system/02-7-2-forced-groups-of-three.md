## 7.2 Forced groups of three

No regex can reliably decide whether a set of three is semantically justified. The linter uses several heuristics and reports warnings.

### `TRI001_THREE_SHORT_PARALLEL_CLAUSES` — warning

Flag sentences containing three comma- or conjunction-separated clauses when:

- each clause is 2–8 tokens
- the clauses begin with the same part of speech or repeated word
- the sentence includes no source-defined three-part set

Example:

> “You will explore circuits, master measurements, and unlock troubleshooting.”

### `TRI002_THREE_DECORATIVE_ADJECTIVES` — warning

Flag noun phrases with three coordinated adjectives, especially abstract or promotional adjectives.

Example:

> “a clear, powerful, and intuitive model”

### `TRI003_THREE_RHETORICAL_EXAMPLES` — warning

Flag “from X to Y to Z”, “whether X, Y, or Z”, or “X, Y, and Z” when each item is a broad example and the plan does not identify a factual closed set.

### `TRI004_EXACTLY_THREE_LIST_ITEMS` — info

An exactly three-item list is not automatically wrong. Report only when:

- all items are short fragments
- the list is introduced by promotional language
- nearby sections repeatedly use three-item lists
- the plan contains a different number of required points

### `TRI005_REPEATED_TRIADS` — error

Three or more triad warnings in 500 words become an error. This catches habitual rhythm even when any individual case might be defensible.

### Factual-three allowlist

Do not warn when metadata identifies a closed set of three, such as:

- three terminals on a named component
- three laws or stages defined by the source
- three answer choices generated by a fixed assessment template
- RGB colour channels
- past, present, and future in a direct grammar explanation

The content plan supplies:

```ts
interface ClosedSetDeclaration {
  label: string;
  members: string[];
  sourceId?: string;
}
```

## 7.3 Canned openings and conclusions

### `CAN001_CANNED_OPENING` — error

Case-insensitive phrase candidates:

- “In today’s fast-paced world”
- “In an ever-evolving world”
- “Let’s dive in”
- “Let’s delve into”
- “Welcome to the world of”
- “Have you ever wondered”
- “Imagine a world where”
- “At its core” at the start of a response
- “Understanding X is crucial/essential”
- “This comprehensive guide”

Some phrases can be legitimate inside source quotation. Ignore quoted spans and citations.

### `CAN002_CANNED_CONCLUSION` — warning

Candidates:

- “In conclusion”
- “To sum up”
- “Ultimately” as an unsupported final flourish
- “By understanding X, you can…”
- “The possibilities are endless”
- “This will empower you to…”
- “As you continue your journey…”

### `CAN003_META_GUIDE_LANGUAGE` — warning

Flag:

- “In this section, we will…”
- “This article explores…”
- “Below, we’ll cover…”

Allow in genuine navigation or long documents when it saves the reader time.

---

## 7.4 Praise and conversational filler

### `PRA001_GENERIC_PRAISE` — error in feedback, warning elsewhere

Candidates:

- “Great question”
- “Excellent question”
- “Fantastic work”
- “Amazing job”
- “You’ve nailed it”
- “Absolutely!” at the start
- “You’re absolutely right”
- “That’s a brilliant observation”

### `PRA002_UNSUPPORTED_ON_TRACK` — warning

Flag “You’re on the right track” unless the next sentence names the evidence.

### `PRA003_REPEATED_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT` — warning

More than one acknowledgement phrase in a feedback response under 200 words.

---

## 7.5 Inflated or promotional language

### `HYP001_INFLATED_IMPORTANCE` — warning

Flag clusters involving words such as:

- crucial
- pivotal
- transformative
- groundbreaking
- powerful
- profound
- remarkable
- invaluable
- indispensable
- revolutionary

A single use may be factually justified. Two or more in 300 words is an error unless protected by quotation.

### `HYP002_CORPORATE_AI_VOCABULARY` — warning

Candidates include:

- leverage as a verb where “use” works
- unlock
- empower
- robust
- seamless
- landscape
- ecosystem
- holistic
- dynamic
- multifaceted
- navigate as an abstract metaphor
- delve
- foster
- realm
- tapestry
- testament
- underscores

Do not ban a word when it is the precise domain term. `ecosystem` in biology and `landscape` in geography may be exact.

### `HYP003_GRAND_ANALOGY` — warning

Flag claims such as “the heart of”, “the backbone of”, or “the cornerstone of” when used for ordinary components or concepts.

---

## 7.6 Formatting and structure

### `FMT001_EM_DASH_DENSITY` — error

- more than two em dashes in 300 words
- more than one em dash in a response under 120 words

A hyphen or minus sign does not count.

### `FMT002_HEADING_DENSITY` — warning

More than one heading per 180 words in lesson or feedback prose. UI section labels are excluded.

### `FMT003_BOLD_DENSITY` — warning

More than four bold spans per 300 words or bold applied to whole sentences.

### `FMT004_FRAGMENT_LIST` — warning

A list of short fragments where one ordinary sentence would be clearer.

### `FMT005_SINGLE_ITEM_SECTION` — warning

A heading followed by one short sentence before another heading.

### `FMT006_REPEATED_SUMMARY` — error

Detect a closing paragraph with high semantic overlap against earlier text and summary cues.

A simple implementation may use token-set Jaccard overlap. Do not require embeddings.

---

## 7.7 Repetition and rhythm

### `REP001_REPEATED_TRANSITION` — warning

More than one use of the same transition in 300 words, especially:

- Additionally
- Moreover
- Furthermore
- Importantly
- Interestingly
- Consequently

### `REP002_REPEATED_SENTENCE_OPENING` — warning

Three consecutive sentences beginning with the same first one or two normalised tokens.

### `REP003_PROMPT_RESTATEMENT` — warning

The response repeats most of the learner’s question before answering. Use token overlap plus a beginning-of-response window.

### `REP004_REDUNDANT_RECAP` — warning

Final sentence restates the previous sentence with no new content.

---

## 7.8 Vagueness and jargon

### `CLR001_VAGUE_REFERENCE` — warning

Flag clusters of “this”, “that”, “it”, or “these” where the referent is unclear.

### `CLR002_UNEXPLAINED_TERM` — warning

A required glossary term appears for the first time without definition, visual cue, or known-concept record.

### `CLR003_NOMINALISATION_DENSITY` — info or warning

Flag dense clusters of abstract nouns ending in `-tion`, `-ment`, `-ance`, `-ity`, or `-ness`. This is a weak heuristic and must not be used alone to reject text.

### `CLR004_UNGROUNDED_EXAMPLE` — warning

An example contains invented values, people, places, or devices that are not labelled as illustrative and do not support the concept.

---

## 7.9 Answer leakage

These rules are contextual and receive the active question package.

### `ANS001_FINAL_ANSWER_IN_HINT` — error

Detect exact or normalised answer tokens in a hint that is not allowed to reveal them.

### `ANS002_ALL_REQUIRED_STEPS_IN_HINT` — error

A model review checks whether the hint supplies every operation needed to obtain the answer.

### `ANS003_REVEAL_PHRASE` — warning

Flag phrases such as “the answer is”, “you should get”, or “this gives us” in Coach and early Assisted hints.

### `ANS004_MULTIPLE_CHOICE_ELIMINATION_LEAK` — warning

A hint must not eliminate every distractor except the correct option.

### `ANS005_EQUIVALENT_NUMERIC_ANSWER` — error

Detect a hidden numeric answer expressed in an equivalent unit, such as `50 mA` when the protected answer is `0.05 A`.

# 8. Linter architecture

## 8.1 Package layout

```text
packages/writing-engine/src/
├─ index.ts
├─ types.ts
├─ tokenizer.ts
├─ protected-spans.ts
├─ rules/
│  ├─ negative-parallelism.ts
│  ├─ triads.ts
│  ├─ canned.ts
│  ├─ praise.ts
│  ├─ hype.ts
│  ├─ formatting.ts
│  ├─ repetition.ts
│  ├─ clarity.ts
│  └─ answer-leakage.ts
├─ metrics.ts
├─ preservation.ts
└─ fixtures/
```

## 8.2 Protected spans

Before linting, mark:

- code fences
- inline code
- equations
- URLs
- citations
- direct quotations
- source titles
- user-provided quoted text

Most style rules ignore protected spans. Answer leakage and factual preservation may still inspect them contextually.

## 8.3 Sentence and clause segmentation

Use a maintained sentence segmenter or a conservative custom segmenter. Preserve offsets. Account for:

- decimal numbers
- abbreviations
- units
- initials
- equations
- Markdown

A full NLP stack is optional. Heuristics must be tested and understandable.

## 8.4 Rule output

Every rule returns zero or more violations. It must never mutate text.

```ts
interface StyleRule {
  id: StyleRuleId;
  defaultSeverity: StyleSeverity;
  run(context: LintContext): StyleViolation[];
}
```

## 8.5 Configuration

```ts
interface WritingProfile {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  maxWordsByContentType: Record<ContentType, number>;
  emDashLimitPer300Words: number;
  headingWordsPerHeading: number;
  allowFriendlyPraise: boolean;
  bannedPhrases: string[];
  warningPhrases: string[];
  disabledRules: StyleRuleId[];
  severityOverrides: Partial<Record<StyleRuleId, StyleSeverity>>;
}
```

The default profile is `george-direct-v1`.

# 9. Passing and scoring

The score is diagnostic. Do not turn prose into a game of maximising an opaque number.

Suggested calculation:

```text
start at 100
- 30 per error
- 8 per warning
- 1 per info item, capped at 5
minimum 0
```

Commit rules:

- zero unexcepted errors
- no preservation failure
- no answer leakage
- no malformed sources
- warnings reviewed for curated content
- generated conversational content may display with warnings when immediate response is more valuable, but the UI must not label it curated

# 10. Targeted editor prompt

Store this in `prompts/style-editor.md`.

```text
You are editing learner-facing text for Discere.

You receive:
- the structured content plan
- the current draft
- exact style violations with character spans
- protected facts, numbers, units, equations, citations, source claims, and
  answer boundaries
- the target learner level and length

Repair the flagged writing with the smallest coherent edits. Do not rewrite
unflagged passages merely to make them sound different.

Remove rhetorical negative parallelisms. Replace forced three-part phrasing
with a content-driven structure. Delete canned introductions, conclusions,
praise, hype, redundant summaries, and decorative transitions.

Use direct sentences and concrete examples. Preserve every protected item.
Do not add new factual claims, examples, analogies, citations, or solution
steps. Do not weaken uncertainty. Do not expose an answer forbidden by the
active learning mode.

Return only the requested JSON schema with:
- revised_text
- an edit record for each violation
- any violation that could not be repaired safely
- a list of protected items you checked
```

# 11. Lesson writer prompt

Store this in `prompts/lesson-writer.md`.

```text
Write one lesson beat from the supplied content plan and visual brief.

The learner will see the visual beside the explanation. Refer to visible
features precisely. Develop one main idea. Use the number of supporting
points the content requires. Include a concrete example when the plan
provides one.

Normal explanation length is 120 to 350 words. Do not pad the response to
reach the maximum. Do not begin with an overview of the lesson. Do not end
with a ceremonial summary.

Follow the Discere writing contract. Avoid negative parallelisms,
forced groups of three, canned phrases, generic praise, inflated importance,
corporate language, repeated transitions, excessive headings, and decorative
em dashes.

Do not invent facts or visual details. Keep equations, values, units, source
claims, and answer boundaries exact.

Return the requested structured schema. Do not include hidden reasoning.
```
